## 4. Seniority Bracket — Honest Placement

After filling the gap and shipping the portfolio:

- **Overall realistic placement: solid mid-level GenAI/AI application engineer.**
- **By company tier:**
  - **FAANG / top AI lab (OpenAI, Anthropic, DeepMind, etc.): junior-to-borderline, and you'd likely not pass the loop.** Your DSA, distributed-systems depth, and production-scale war stories won't clear their bar. Realistic outcome: screened out or eaten in the technical loop.
  - **Well-funded mid-tier startup / scale-up: mid-level.** Credible hire for a GenAI app engineer role; you can build their features and deploy them.
  - **Typical SaaS company / digital agency: mid-to-senior.** Relative to their existing talent, your GenAi specialization reads as senior on the AI axis even if you're mid on the systems axis.
  - **Small agency / outsourcing shop: senior / lead.** You'd be one of their strongest GenAI people.
- **Single most common reason an honest hiring manager downgrades you one level:** **no production-operation evidence** — you can show you *built* it, not that you *ran it under real traffic and owned it when it broke*. Portfolios prove construction, not operation, and experienced interviewers probe exactly there.

## 7. Technical Interview Readiness

After filling the gap and shipping portfolios:

- **System design (LLM/RAG/distributed inference/scaling):** You can pass **mid-tier well-funded startup and typical-SaaS** rounds — RAG architecture, agent design, caching/cost tiering, basic scaling. You will **struggle at FAANG/top-lab** depth (multi-region consistency, capacity math, novel large-scale trade-offs). Highest tier you reliably pass: **mid-tier well-funded startup.**
- **ML/AI fundamentals (transformers, training, fine-tuning, eval):** Strong area thanks to the Ng/DL specializations + fine-tuning courses. You can pass up to **mid-tier startup and many senior-SaaS** rounds. At **top labs**, applied-research depth and "explain this paper / derive this" will expose limits. Highest reliable tier: **strong mid-tier / well-funded startup.**
- **Coding rounds (DSA / leetcode):** **Weakest area — nothing in the inventory builds DSA.** Without dedicated grind you fail medium-LeetCode loops. With ~2–3 months of focused practice you clear **typical SaaS and many startup** screens. FAANG-style algorithmic loops remain out of reach without serious, separate preparation. Highest reliable tier (post-grind): **typical SaaS / startup.**
- **Take-home / portfolio review:** **Your strongest interview format.** Production-grade repos with observability, eval, IaC, and a real writeup will impress up to **well-funded startup / scale-up** reviewers. This is where you should steer processes. Highest reliable tier: **well-funded startup.**
- **Behavioral / "tell me about a time":** **Capped by the absence of real production war stories.** You can speak to building and to project decisions, but "tell me about an incident you owned in production / a conflict on your team / a scaling failure" will be thin because you've worked solo. Highest reliable tier: **typical SaaS** (startups probing for ownership-under-fire will notice the gap).

- **Single biggest interview weakness:** the combination of **(a) DSA/coding-round readiness and (b) production war stories.** DSA is the more *fixable* of the two — closeable with 2–3 months of deliberate LeetCode practice. The war-stories gap is only closeable by operating real systems (real clients at scale or a job). If you must pick one to fix first for interview ROI: **grind DSA**, because it's the hard gate that blocks otherwise-passable candidates, and steer every process you can toward **take-home/portfolio review**, your strongest format.
